# Slide Design Principles (Storytelling Layer)

General slide-design principles above the layout mechanics in `SKILL.md`.
Created 2026-07-23 from the Storytelling with Data intake (decisions log:
`reviews/2026-07-23-swd-graph-making-decisions-log.md`). These are principles
to reason with, not templates to copy.

## Before Any Deck: the So-What Discipline

- **Who / What / How, in that order.** Identify the decision-making audience
  (narrow it — "internal and external stakeholders" means nobody), what you
  need them to know or DO (take a confident stance; you are the
  subject-matter expert), and only then how data makes the point.
- **Push Ethan for an outline**: the whole argument boiled down to one
  paragraph, then to one concise statement (the Big Idea: articulate your
  unique point of view, convey what's at stake, one complete sentence).
- **3-minute story test:** if you had three minutes, what would you say? If
  the deck can't be told that way, the structure isn't ready.
- Decks are **explanatory, not exploratory** — present the two pearls, not
  all 100 oysters. Exploratory output is never the deliverable.

## Narrative Structure

- **Front-load the ask.** Live decks state context and the recommendation
  early (SWD 8.12–8.19 builds to a recommendation the audience already
  expects); the closing slide repeats the ask explicitly.
- **Repetition is a feature:** say the key message in the intro, the body,
  and the close. Each pass can compress ("plot → twists → call to action" as
  a one-slide recap).
- **Every written deliverable needs a so-what.** A written report or
  leave-behind without a stated implication is unfinished — state what the
  reader should conclude or do.
- **Live presentation vs slideument:** a deck presented live carries less
  text (the presenter is the words); a document that circulates alone carries
  the full reasoning. Decide which one you're building before writing slides.
  If it must do both, write for the circulating reader and speak past the
  text live.

## Iterative Process (the reference)

`gallery/decks/swd-ch8-price-story/` (SWD 8.1 → 8.19, with 8.20 as the
before/after comparison) is the canonical example of iterating a rough deck
into a good one. When Ethan invokes "iterative process," use this sequence as
the model: declutter → establish hierarchy → emphasize the story → rebuild
titles as takeaways → assemble the narrative arc.

Standing behavior: for every before/after or improvement Ethan flags, study
the before, extract the design decisions that produced the after, and bake
them into the applicable skills. Understand bad slides; don't store them.

## Progressive Reveal / Voiceover Decks

The `gallery/decks/swd-ch9-moonville-progression/` pattern — blank axes
first, then chronological reveal of segments with one annotation per step —
is a valid style for walking an audience through a timeline live. Rarely our
default presentation style, but the logical ordering is sound; reuse the
ordering even when not animating.

## Organization Devices

- **Parent categories** (the book calls these "super-categories"): group long
  lists/tables/axes under labeled parent groups (demographic rows under
  age/income/education; grouped period spans; quadrant titles). They pre-chew
  the structure for the reader.
- **Slide density:** "sparse but good" (the SWD ch8 deck) is acceptable and
  clear; Ethan's house preference runs slightly denser — never sparser at the
  cost of the argument, never denser at the cost of the squint test.
- **Alignment carries hierarchy:** title / legend / axis blocks aligned to
  one upper-left edge, takeaway text right-aligned near the data it explains
  (see `gallery/slides/swd-6.3-*`).
